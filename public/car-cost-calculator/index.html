    How the car ownership cost is calculated

    Annual fuel or charging cost is calculated from your annual distance, consumption and fuel price (distance × consumption ÷ 100 × price in metric units, or distance ÷ mpg × price in US units), so changing how far you drive immediately updates your monthly and yearly totals. Monthly fixed costs (depreciation or loan payments, and parking) are multiplied by 12 and added to yearly insurance, taxes and maintenance.

    Most drivers focus on gas prices and underestimate the rest. With 15,000 km a year at 6.5 l/100 km and $1.80 a liter ($1,755 a year for fuel), plus $350 a month for depreciation or payments, $1,800 a year for insurance and registration, $900 for maintenance and tires and $60 a month for parking and tolls, owning a car adds up to $9,375 a year – $781.25 a month, $0.63 per km and $93,750 over ten years.

    Depreciation is often the single largest cost even when a car is paid off: a $30,000 car worth $12,000 after five years loses $3,600 a year, or $300 every month, just sitting in the driveway. Switching units converts distance, fuel consumption and fuel price automatically so you can compare metric and US figures.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much does it cost to own a car per month?

    Add your yearly fuel cost (from annual distance, consumption and fuel price), insurance, taxes and maintenance to 12 times your monthly depreciation or payment and parking, then divide by 12. With the default US figures, the true cost is $781.25 a month, or $9,375 a year.

    What should I enter for depreciation if my car is paid off?

    Estimate what your car is worth today, subtract what it will likely sell for in a few years, and divide by the number of months in between. For example, a car that drops from $18,000 to $10,800 over three years costs $200 a month in depreciation even with no loan payment.

    Does this calculator work for electric cars (EVs)?

    Yes. In metric units, enter your car’s energy consumption in kWh/100 km (for example 18) and your average charging price per kWh. EVs often have lower energy and routine maintenance costs, though insurance, tires and depreciation still add up.

    What if I invested the money instead?

    The “If you invested it instead” box shows what the same money would grow to if you invested it every month instead of spending it on a car, at a 4% yearly return compounded monthly. At $781.25 a month, that grows to about $114,600 in 10 years – $93,750 of your own money and the rest returns. Taxes, fees and inflation are left out. It is an example, not investment advice.
